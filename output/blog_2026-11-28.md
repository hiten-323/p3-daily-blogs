## How do people brew instant coffee in Indian cities?

Walk into any apartment kitchen in Bangalore, Mumbai, or Delhi at seven in the morning and you will hear the same rhythm: kettle clicking off, a spoon clinking against a steel tumbler, the quick swirl before the first sip. Most people start with a heaped spoonful of powder, pour a thin stream of hot water just to wet it, then top it off with boiled milk and sugar to taste. Some swear by the "decoction" method — dissolving the coffee in a tiny stainless cup first so it emulsifies properly before the milk hits it. Others keep it simple: granules straight into the mug, milk poured hot from the pan, stirred fast so a thin layer of foam settles on top. The trick nobody talks about is water temperature — just off the boil, not rolling, or the coffee turns sharp. A 100g jar at ₹309 lasts a household weeks, and the 50g at ₹209 fits a solo drinker's monthly rhythm.
